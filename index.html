    How the estimate works

    Soil data. The clicked point is sent to USDA NRCS Soil Data Access (SSURGO). It returns the mapped soil unit, its component soils and their share of the unit, and each soil's layers down to about 2 m: sand and clay percentages, bulk density, water held at field capacity and at wilting point, saturation-extract salinity (EC) and cation exchange capacity (CEC). Missing values are filled with defaults, and the data line under the readout lists which ones. A layer counts as rock only when the survey says so (bedrock, cemented or indurated layers). Organic layers (muck, peat) get organic-soil values. A layer with no texture at all is treated as loam and flagged.

    No data at a spot. Urban land, gravel pits and a few unsurveyed areas have no soil data. The data line shows how much of the map unit has usable data. When none does, the page checks rings of 8 points at 0.3, 0.9, 2.5, 6, 16 and 37 miles, stops once it finds enough real soil, and averages those points weighted by 1/distance² (conductivity on a log scale).

    Water. Map units of water are treated as salt water when the coastline puts the spot on the sea side, or when the name says ocean, bay or tidal; otherwise fresh water (5 mS/m, εr 80). Buttons under the readout let you correct it.

    Salt water nearby. The page includes the OpenStreetMap coastline for the US (accurate to about 100 m; the Great Lakes and inland salt lakes are not included). After each lookup it finds the nearest salt water and casts a line every 5° out to 37 miles. For each antenna and angle it estimates the stretch of ground where the signal reflects (the first Fresnel zone along that line) and blends salt water into the reflection for the share of that stretch lying over the sea. This is a simplified model. The direction buttons compare the best direction across the water with directions over land. The σ and εr readout is always the soil under the antenna.

    Moisture. Bone dry is 40% of the wilting-point water content, Dry is the wilting point, Moist is halfway to field capacity, Wet is field capacity (a soaked soil after it drains), Saturated is 90% of the pore space filled. The same condition is applied to every layer.

    Dielectric constant. CRIM mixing of mineral grains (εr 4.7), water (εr 80) and air, using porosity from bulk density. It gives values similar to the Topp equation.

    Conductivity. An Archie-type model: pore-water conductivity (from the lab EC, concentrated as the soil dries) times a porosity and water-content factor, plus a surface-conduction term from clay (CEC). Non-saline soils get a floor of 0.25 dS/m for pore water.

    Skin depth and band. The skin depth is where the radio field has faded to about 37% of its surface strength. It is not a cutoff; deeper soil still contributes, less and less. Layers are weighted by how much power reaches them, and the deepest mapped layer is assumed to continue downward. Bedrock and cemented layers are treated as limestone or caliche (about 1.5 mS/m, εr 7).

    Frequency. In this model each layer's σ and εr do not change with frequency. Changing band only changes the depth weighting and the reflection math, so a uniform soil shows the same σ and εr on every band. Real soils do vary somewhat with frequency (see N6LF's measurements at antennasbyn6lf.com); that is not modeled.

    Antenna summary. The gain table assumes flat, open ground. It combines each antenna's free-space pattern (¼λ vertical 5.15 dBi over perfect ground, ½λ dipole 2.15 dBi, dipoles broadside) with the Fresnel reflection off ground with these constants, at 5°, 10° and 20°. The vertical row assumes enough radials that loss near the antenna is small; it covers the far-field reflection only. Hills, slopes, buildings and trees are not included.

    Accuracy. These estimates have not been checked against HF ground measurements. Treat them as a starting point for modeling, not as measured values. For real numbers, measure the site with a VNA ground probe (N6LF's method) and enter them under "Use my own ground values".

    Reference values: Poor (dry sand) about 1 mS/m, εr 5. Average about 5 mS/m, εr 13. Good (moist farmland) about 30 mS/m, εr 20. Salt water 5000 mS/m, εr 81.
